## Question 1 — Would you purchase IAM today?

**If you were the CEO of a distribution company evaluating IAM, would you purchase this product today?**

### Answer: **Not an immediate full purchase — Yes to a paid limited pilot / proof-of-value.**

**Reasoning**
- Post-EEP, IAM finally presents as a **weekly management system**: capacity (OEC), Active Priorities, Waiting, WIP, Deferred, and plain-English Trust Pyramid.
- That is enough to justify a **pilot investment** with clear success criteria.
- It is not yet enough for an immediate enterprise license commitment because:
  1. Scoring is still explicitly interim (SD-1).
  2. Security/RBAC is not production-grade (Phase 6).
  3. Outcome proof / “we made money” loop is thinner than ERP/analytics buyers expect after month one.
  4. Visual/commercial packaging still trails modern SaaS decision products.

A rational CEO buys **learning and operating rhythm** now, and **platform commitment** after 4–8 weeks of Monday evidence.

## Question 2 — Three improvements that would convert to purchase

If the answer is not an immediate “Yes,” the three most important improvements that would convince a purchase:

1. **Proven weekly outcomes** — Clear expected vs actual (or at least completed-impact narrative) on Recently Completed / Outcome Validation so the CEO can show ROI to ownership.  
2. **Final or confidently bounded scoring story** — Either close SD-1 or package interim scoring so buyers feel safe defending ranks without a consultant.  
3. **Real identity & accountability** — People directory + authentication/RBAC (Phase 6) so Assign/Accept feels like company process, not a pilot form with user ids.

Honorable mention (would accelerate but not #1–3 alone): premium visual meeting mode; qualitative “if we wait” when COI is null; side-panel drill-down.

| Lens | Rank vs category | Comment |

| **Executive experience (weekly decision ritual)** | **Above typical ERP screens; competitive with specialized mid-market decision tools** | OEC + Active Priorities is a clearer executive model than most ERP “exception queues.” |

**Overall commercial position:** A **differentiated weekly inventory action OS** for distributors — no longer an internal console, not yet a category-defining commercial product. Closest peer set: decision-support / action-management overlays — not NetSuite screen clones or Tableau workbooks.

## Question 4 — Application vs management decision support system?

**Has IAM evolved from a software application into a true management decision support system?**

### Answer: **Yes — for the weekly executive decision loop. Not yet — as a complete enterprise decision platform.**

**Why “yes” for the weekly loop**
- Forces **finite commitments** (OEC) instead of infinite ranked lists.
- Separates **commit / wait / defer / execute**.
- Provides **explainability for action** (Trust Pyramid), not only scores.
- Supports **governance** (Approve/Defer/Reject/Assign/Accept/Pause) as management acts.
- Produces a **meeting agenda** (Summary, Momentum, Decisions, Active Priorities).

**Why “not yet” as a full MDS**
- Limited organizational learning / outcome analytics.
- Interim scoring science.
- Identity, audit depth for enterprise, and multi-role security incomplete.
- Emergency and some lifecycle edges still stubbed or multi-step.

**Net:** EEP is the moment IAM crossed from “application with recommendations” to “management decision support for Monday.” That is a real category shift — still early in the category.
